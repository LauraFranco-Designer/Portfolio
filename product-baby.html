Redesign Philips Avent Baby Monitor

An intergation of health related features for baby care

This project aimed to redesign the Philips Avent Baby Monitor to enhance usability and improve the overall user experience for parents. Through in-depth user research, usability testing, and iterative prototyping, the design process focused on simplifying interactions and addressing user pain points. In addition to improving core functionality, the redesign also explored the integration of new health-related features not present in the original device. These insights were translated into practical design recommendations to create a more inclusive and intuitive product, including updates to the companion app and packaging.

As part of Philips' broader product portfolio, the Avent Baby Monitor was selected for a usability-driven redesign. The client wanted to evaluate the current experience and identifying opportunities to enhance both product and app interactions. The core question was: How can we improve the usability and perceived value of the Philips Avent Baby Monitor for modern parents while integrating meaningful health functionalities that go beyond the original offering? The challenge involved not only improving the monitor’s interaction design but also rethinking its role in supporting health awareness in everyday caregiving routines.

Research Process

The research prompted the design team to address key issues around privacy, ethics, data use, and baby safety. A major concern was the impact of constant camera recording and potential misuse, such as parents monitoring caregivers excessively, which could harm trust. They also considered dependency risks, while the monitor offers peace of mind and freedom, it might cause parents to feel the need to stay always connected, leading to stress. Sharing health data required balancing helpful information without overwhelming or worrying parents unnecessarily. Finally, providing a device that should support, but never replace, real caregiver attention, especially regarding a child’s safety. The research used various approaches to gather input for the device redesign.

  • First-hand experience

    One team member, unfamiliar with the product, performed the first observation while others watched. It began with unboxing and followed a series of scenarios designed to cover the full interaction flow (from setup to device connection)

  • Analysis of the reviews

    Current users share their opinions in forums and review sections, often focusing on the product’s impact on their lives. The team summarizes key insights from Amazon and Philips reviews, noting the potential lack of objectivity

  • Compact Field Study

    A survey was conducted with seven parents who currently use or recently used baby monitors. Participants had babies or toddlers (up to 4 years old), ensuring recent experience. They provided insights on the pros and cons of various monitors to better understand the users and context

  • Usability Inspection

    A cognitive walkthrough (n=1) and heuristic evaluation were conducted to deepen product understanding before user testing. The walkthrough helped identify key elements and informed test setup. For the heuristic evaluation, the team focused on 6 of Jakob Nielsen’s 10 principles, emphasizing feedback and user control

  • User Tests

    Building on previous insights, a user test was conducted with five mothers to observe real-world use and identify current issues. Scenario-based tasks revealed user behavior and experiences, helping the team compare findings and define the project’s core problem

Key Insights

Based on the findings from the research phase, the primary focus areas were defined to ensure a seamless and cohesive product-service experience. These areas include improving physical interaction, clarifying information architecture, enhancing screen UI consistency, and unifying interaction patterns. Users encountered issues such as hidden or unclear functions, inconsistent feedback, poor UI hierarchy, and fragmented interaction flows, emphasizing the need for a more intuitive and integrated design.

Supporting improvements included enhancing the phone application to enable additional health related features and simplifying the package and manual for a smoother onboarding experience. These secondary areas helped reinforce the core design changes by improving accessibility and clarity throughout the product ecosystem.

Design Goal

The redesign aims to optimize and simplify the light indicators on both the parent and camera units by incorporating light strips on top of the devices. Animations and color variations will help users understand device status from a distance and all angles. Illuminated buttons are intended to provide clear feedback on user inputs, while an onboarding screen with a pre-setup feature will streamline button interactions for key functions. Additionally, the packaging will be redesigned to enhance the unboxing experience and support a smooth, intuitive setup process

App Redesign

The redesign aims to optimize and simplify device status indicators to improve visibility and user understanding from all angles and distances. It seeks to enhance feedback on user interactions and streamline the onboarding process for easier operation of key functions. Additionally, the redesign aims to improve the unboxing experience, making setup more intuitive and seamless for users.

The app is organized into three main areas: the camera view, which offers features similar to the baby monitor; the analytics section, focusing on medical insights; and the user profile

  • Live Camera

  • Analytics

  • User Profile

Interviews and literature reviews revealed that while parents value health tracking, excessive data can cause unnecessary stress. To address this, we focused on simple, non-intrusive features. Parents also prefer solutions that don’t require attaching anything to the baby, so we chose to enhance the existing camera and parent unit using sensors applied to the camera itself. The aim is to provide supportive insights, not medical diagnostics, that complement parental care without overwhelming it.

Unlike the monitor, the app includes a dedicated section called “Analytics,” focused on the baby’s health. This area doesn’t allow control of features like talk-back or night light, as the focus is on monitoring rather than interaction. The camera view remains visible but is minimized to highlight key health data such as live breathing, sleep tracking, temperature, and motion. Parents can view real-time data, daily overviews, and history from previous days, helping them stay informed without feeling overwhelmed.

Device Redesign

To improve ease of use, the team focused on helping parents quickly check the baby’s status, even from across the room. In the original design, the screen on the parent unit was hard to read from a distance. In the redesign, we introduced a light strip on the parent unit, making status updates visible from any angle. A similar light strip was added to the top of the camera unit. We also simplified multiple indicators into a single, intuitive system using color and animation to communicate the device’s status clearly. The strip on the camera unit also performs as the night light, and the unit is made more compact by saving space for the lamp.

Three buttons are located on the top-right corner of the parent unit to be easily approachable. They are used as ‘Hot keys’ to operate functions within one click. With the half-transparent material, they are lightened up to give suggestions or indicate the active status. This gives the user more confidence while using the monitor and feeling in control.

The clip-on on the back of the monitor gives the user the possibility to wear the monitor on their clothes. The clip-on will make the monitor more reliable because the user can keep it close at all times while still being ergonomic.

Packaging Redesign

The redesigned packaging uses arrows to clearly indicate the sliding directions of the two compartments, making it easier for users to understand how to open the box and recognize that it contains two separate components. This helps users quickly identify which adapter belongs to which unit. An NFC logo on the packaging guides users to place their phone on a specific spot. As the compartments are removed, a phone-sized slot is revealed, naturally inviting the user to place their phone there. This triggers the NFC reader, directing them to the app download page—ensuring that users become aware of the app immediately upon unboxing.

Evaluation & Reflection

The final redesign was evaluated through user testing with parents and students using prototypes, interviews, and questionnaires. This phase confirmed that several key improvements positively impacted usability and user experience.

  • Improved Onboarding & Setup

    The updated setup process, supported by clearer guidance and intuitive UI, was found to be much more accessible than relying on a physical manual.

  • Feedback & LED Light Strip

    Illuminated buttons gave users clear feedback on their interactions, improving trust and usability. Replacing multiple indicators with a single light strip improved status visibility and made feedback more immediate and natural for parents.

  • Simplified Interface

    A restructured menu and clearer button placement made the product easier to understand and use.

The evaluation of the redesign also highlighted some areas for improvment

  • Clip-on Attachment

    Though useful in theory, the clip-on’s current design needs further optimization for comfort and practicality.

  • Setup Flow

    Some features may be better introduced during setup rather than later exploration, to reduce confusion.

  • Intrusiveness of the App

    While the app provides added value, its health tracking features and data presentation can feel intrusive. A more balanced and less overwhelming approach is needed.

While keeping in mind the limitations and areas for future improvement, we can confidently say that the redesign of the Philips Avent Baby Monitor has achieved its design goal. Under key aspects like improved reliability, intuitive usability, and stronger support for parents, the project delivered meaningful enhancements. The addition of a LED light strip for clearer feedback, a more accessible onboarding process, and simplified user interaction all contributed to a more parent-friendly experience. Despite some elements still needing refinement, the redesigned system shows strong alignment with user needs and expectations.
